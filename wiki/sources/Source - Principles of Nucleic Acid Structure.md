# Source - Principles of Nucleic Acid Structure

* **Location**: `Structural Biology Tutorials/Principles_of_nucleic_acid_structure.pdf`
* **Focus**: Biophysical principles of DNA and RNA architecture, conformational flexibility, base pairing/stacking thermodynamics, and macromolecular recognition.

---

## 📌 Core Structural Principles

1. **Conformational Polymorphism of DNA**:
   * **B-DNA**: Canonical right-handed helix (10.5 bp/turn, rise of 3.4 Å/bp), wide major groove accessible to transcription factors.
   * **A-DNA/RNA**: Right-handed, compact (11 bp/turn), deep and narrow major groove typical of RNA duplexes and DNA-RNA hybrids.
   * **Z-DNA**: Left-handed zig-zag conformation favored by alternating purine-pyrimidine tracts under high torsional strain.
2. **Forces Stabilizing Double Helices**:
   * **Base Stacking (Hydrophobic & $\pi$-$\pi$ interactions)**: Dominant thermodynamic contributor to duplex stability.
   * **Watson-Crick Hydrogen Bonding**: Provides fidelity and sequence specificity ($A=T$ with 2 H-bonds, $G\equiv C$ with 3 H-bonds).
3. **DNA-Protein Interface Mechanics**:
   * Transcription factors (e.g. [[TP53]]) read genetic information via direct base contacts (hydrogen bonding with major groove hydrogen bond donors/acceptors) and shape recognition (minor groove width and electrostatic potential).

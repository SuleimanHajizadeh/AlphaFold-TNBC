## 📌 Chemical Mechanism of Kinase Catalysis

Phosphoryl transfer from ATP to substrate hydroxyl groups (Ser, Thr, Tyr) is catalyzed by protein kinases through a coordinated geometric arrangement:

```
                  Substrate - O - H  (Nucleophile)
                          │
                          ▼ (In-line Attack)
                 O       O
                 ║       ║
         Ad-O-P-O-P - O - P - O⁻  (ATP γ-Phosphate)
              │   │   │   │
              O⁻  O⁻  O⁻  O⁻
                  ▲   ▲
                  │   │
                 Mg²⁺ Mg²⁺  (Divalent Catalytic Cations)
                  │   │
          Asp (DFG)  Asn (Catalytic Loop)
```

1. **In-line Nucleophilic Attack**:
   * The substrate Ser/Thr hydroxyl group acts as the attacking nucleophile on the $\gamma$-phosphate of ATP.
   * Proceeding via a trigonal bipyramidal pentacoordinate transition state.
2. **General Base Catalysis**:
   * The conserved **Aspartate in the HRD motif** acts as a catalytic general base, deprotonating the substrate hydroxyl group to generate a reactive oxyanion nucleophile.
3. **Divalent Metal ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$) Coordination**:
   * The conserved **Aspartate in the DFG motif** ([[Kinase Activation Loop & Allostery]]) coordinates one or two $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions, neutralizing negative charge buildup and correctly orienting the $\beta$- and $\gamma$-phosphates of ATP.
4. **Transition State Stabilization by Conserved Lysine**:
   * The invariant catalytic **Lysine** in the $\beta3$-strand forms a salt bridge with the conserved **Glutamate** on the $\alpha\text{C}$-helix, stabilizing the $\alpha/\beta$-phosphate oxygens.
